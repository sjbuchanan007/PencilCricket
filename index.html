Pencil Cricket

Roll for runs. Appeal on OWZTHAT. Batting stays in until all out.

Pass the device to the other player between innings. No actual pencils were harmed.

How a ball is played

  • The batting side rolls the Batting Die: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, or OWZTHAT.
  • A number is runs scored off that ball — play carries straight on.
  • OWZTHAT is an appeal. The bowling side rolls the Bowling Die to answer it: Bowled, Stumped, Caught, Not Out, No Ball, or L.B.W.
  • Not Out — the appeal fails, no runs, batting continues.
  • No Ball — the appeal fails and the batting side gets 1 run.
  • Anything else (Bowled, Stumped, Caught, L.B.W.) is a wicket.
  • An innings ends when the wicket limit falls (10, by default). Each side bats one innings; the higher score wins. Equal scores is a Tie.
  • There's no built-in over limit — pick "Limit overs" at setup if you want a fixed-length house-rule match instead of playing to all out.

Rules adapted from Owzthat, the dice cricket game invented by Lindop's in 1924 and patented in 1932.
